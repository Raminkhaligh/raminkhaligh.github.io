Rescuing Market Share from the GMS Sanctions Crisis

Role: National Rep → Senior PM Product: Huawei Mobile Services (HMS) Tech: HMS SDK, Device OS Detection API

Sanctions stripped Google Mobile Services (GMS) from Huawei devices overnight. Essential super apps crashed, and hardware sales dropped. As the sole liaison between Huawei and the country's top tech companies, I ran the technical and strategic rescue.

The TL;DR: Losing GMS was an existential threat to Huawei's local market. By opening direct C-level talks with Snapp (the region's top super app) and Cafe Bazaar (the leading app store), we shipped a two-part fix: native HMS SDKs to stop the app crashes, and a dynamic OS-detection backend that removed download friction for millions of users.

01. The Problem Space

A Hardware Business Built on Borrowed Software

Losing Google Mobile Services to international sanctions wasn't a software glitch. It was an immediate threat to a multi-million-dollar hardware distribution business.

Snapp (the country's most-used super app for ride-hailing and daily errands) started crashing on every new Huawei phone. Without Google Maps and Google Play Services, local apps couldn't render locations or push notifications.

Market share dropped fast. Regional sellers and importers began refusing to stock Huawei devices because customers were returning them. As the National Representative for HMS, I was the one point of contact holding a hardware business together while it waited on localized software compatibility.

02. The Strategy

C-Level Diplomacy Meets API Engineering

The Dual-Pronged Rescue Mission

I had to bridge Huawei's global engineering capabilities with the localized technical roadmaps of the region's biggest tech companies, acting at once as negotiator, technical product owner, and crisis manager.

The goal: first, stop the bleeding by working directly with Snapp's CPO, CTO, and CMO to replace hardcoded Google APIs with Huawei's native SDKs. Second, secure long-term usability by partnering with Cafe Bazaar on a frictionless app-discovery path for non-GMS devices.

03. The Architecture

Engineering the Non-Google Ecosystem

A technical overhaul turned Huawei phones from broken to fully supported, using custom detection infrastructure and localized cloud services.

Step 1 — The Snapp SDK Overhaul

I was the single point of contact between Huawei and Snapp's leadership. We integrated Huawei Location Kit and Map Kit in place of the broken Google equivalents and shipped a stable, HMS-native version of the super app.

Step 2 — OS Detection Infrastructure

Users couldn't be expected to figure out compatibility on their own. Working with Cafe Bazaar, I specified a backend detection service: when a user opened the store, it read the device string and identified whether the phone was GMS-enabled or HMS-only.

Step 3 — The Dedicated Huawei Hub

Once an HMS phone was detected, Cafe Bazaar routed the user to a custom landing page that listed only apps vetted for HMS compatibility, removing download friction and crash reports at the source.

Step 4 — The AppGallery Milestone

Building on the Snapp integration, I led the deployment to Huawei's native AppGallery: the first Iranian application published on the global Huawei store, and the reference implementation every local company that followed used.
